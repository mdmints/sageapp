# Sage — AI-Powered Women's Health Research Platform

**Evidence-based women's health**

> I built this because smart, health-conscious women deserve better than making decisions based on the loudest voice in their feed. Women's health has been underleveraged and underresearched for decades, and the noise online has made it harder, not easier, to find the truth. The research exists and Sage is just a better front door to it.

---

## Who it's for

Women aged 25 to 55 who are research-curious but time-poor. She has Googled a supplement at midnight, ended up on a Reddit thread, watched three TikToks, and come away more confused than when she started.

While Sage is women-focused, anyone navigating the wellness space, partners, caregivers, older adults, will find value in a tool that replaces noise with evidence.

---

## The problem

Women's health is visually and emotionally nuanced in ways most health tools don't acknowledge. The NIH didn't require studies to include female participants until 1993. The arrival of social media has blurred the line between evidence and anecdote at scale, in formats designed to be emotionally compelling rather than accurate.

Meanwhile the wellness industry has never been louder. A supplement can go from unknown to viral overnight, on the back of anecdote, not evidence. The problem is not a lack of information. It is a lack of trustworthy, relevant information that was actually written with women in mind.

---

## The solution

Search any supplement, paste a TikTok, or drop in a claim. Sage returns a structured verdict scored across four dimensions: clinical evidence, safety profile, expert consensus, and how well the research represents women. Every result includes a plain-English bottom line and citations back to the source.

The personalization layer means a 28-year-old with PCOS and a 52-year-old in perimenopause receive meaningfully different verdicts for the same supplement. Where the research distinguishes between them, Sage surfaces it. 

Early user feedback was consistently strong around two things: the freedom to search anything, a product, a trend, a viral claim, and the feeling that results were speaking to them specifically. The Verdict feature, which lets users paste a TikTok or post directly for fact-checking, resonated as something genuinely new.

---

## Key decisions

**Research depth over conversational AI**
A chat interface was considered and set aside. The risk with chat is the experience becomes about the conversation rather than the verdict. The research should speak clearly and directly over open-ended dialogue.

**Personalization over community**
User reviews and shared stacks were deliberately skipped. Community introduces the anecdotal noise Sage exists to cut through. If it arrives later, it will live clearly separated from the evidence layer.

**Verdict clarity over exhaustive data**
Most users don't need more data. They need better interpretation of the data that exists. The plain-English bottom line, "What to expect," and "Is this worth trying for you?" matter more than comprehensive tables.

## What was not built

- Conversational AI interface
- Community or social layer
- Supplement purchasing or affiliate integration
- Symptom tracker
- Wearable and health record integration 
- Provider Bridge 

---

## What's next

**My Hub: deeper data integration**
Connecting Oura Ring, Apple Health, health records, and biometrics would allow research to be filtered by what your body is actually doing, not just your stated goals. The data model was built with this in mind.

**Provider Bridge: the feature I'm most excited about**
Women often arrive at appointments without the language or organized evidence to advocate for themselves. Provider Bridge lets a user compile everything she has been researching in Sage and share it with her doctor, as a structured PDF or email, with suggested questions generated from her research.

**Practitioner partnerships**
Sage is not a replacement for clinical care. But Provider Bridge directly enables a longer-term opportunity: a clinical interface that makes Sage useful on both sides of the appointment.
